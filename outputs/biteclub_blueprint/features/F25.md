# F25 — Your Take

## Strategy
Your Take makes a friend’s food idea actionable and gives people an optional way to show what they changed. Its primary value is a usable personal version; sharing is a separate decision. Ship after Make Mine, recipe provenance, and the Share Composer. Measure completed personal adaptations and voluntary shares, without rewarding public activity over private cooking.

## Experience and screen design
From `POST` or `RECIPE`, “Make Mine” opens `ADAPT`, then `VARIANT`. After cooking, `MEAL_DONE` offers “Save my version” and a secondary “Share my take.” `CAPTURE` accepts only the user’s chosen new media. `REVIEW_ATTACHMENT` shows whether a recipe may be attached. `POST` displays a short authored change note, such as “Used cucumber for crunch,” and a source chip only where the viewer may access it. There is no automatic repost of the original picture, caption, or recipe.

## Production sequence
The adaptation flow creates an owned variant through `POST /v1/plans/{planId}/adaptations` using the accessible immutable source version. Saving the result uses the cookbook API. Sharing creates a draft with `POST /v1/post-drafts`, a stable `clientDraftId`, own caption/media, `sourcePostId`, and eligible `attachment.planId` with confirmed changes. The server verifies source access and derives permissible provenance; the TAKE classification is internal, not a client authority flag. F31’s idempotent `POST /v1/posts` transaction creates the post and lineage edge, then emits `take.published` and `post.published` in one outbox. The UI opens the canonical resulting post after any retry.

## Data and API contracts
`Variant(id,ownerId,sourceRecipeVersionId,recipeVersionId,changeSummary,rightsOrigin,version)` records user-confirmed changes. `RemixEdge(childPostId,parentPostId,sourceRecipeVersionId,createdAt)` cannot point to the same post or create a cycle. Rights are server-derived, never a client boolean. Owned user recipes and appropriately licensed catalog sources may be publishable. A third-party saved recipe grants private use, so its take may share the user’s own image and original change note but cannot publish the full inherited recipe text. Invalid source or variant ownership returns `SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE` or `VARIANT_NOT_OWNED`.

## Permissions and lifecycle
The new post receives an explicit audience and save policy; neither is inherited. Its own media belongs to its author and can be shared only through that explicit audience. Source access is checked independently for each viewer. A deleted/private source becomes a neutral unavailable source label with no identity leak. An existing authorized recipe copy may continue supporting private adaptation after the source post expires; it does not restore access to source media.

## Failure and offline behavior
Draft a change note and select local media offline. Publishing waits for source/rights revalidation and upload readiness. If source access disappears, preserve the private variant and allow a standalone post of owned content after explicit preview. Do not silently drop attribution from a publish already queued. Retry with the original idempotency key and payload hash.

## Integration and dependencies
F01, F19, F24, F26, F30, F31, and F40. Memory or hard exclusions can inform a private adaptation, but their values never enter the public change summary automatically.

## Execution plan
Build variant provenance validation, then the post-cook invitation and own-media draft path. Implement permission-aware source chips and transactionally created lineage. Track take-publication failures and prohibited attachment attempts by reason code.

## Acceptance and release gates
Test source deletion during publishing, recipe-copy versus media rights, two-device retries, blocked source authors, and an audience wider than the source. `social.takes.enabled` disables sharing entry points while private variants and cookbook saves remain functional. Release requires no source-media reuse and no unapproved recipe republication.
