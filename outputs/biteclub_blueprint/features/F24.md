# F24 — Recipe Attachments

## Strategy
A food post should offer a useful path to cooking when its author has recipe details to share. An attachment is a versioned recipe object, not text extracted from a photo. Posting a photo without a recipe remains valid. Ship before recipe saving, Make Mine from posts, and Ask for Recipe.

## Experience and screen design
`ATTACH_RECIPE` lets the author choose an eligible owned recipe, approved catalog recipe, or enter their own recipe. `REVIEW_ATTACHMENT` previews ingredients, quantities, units, steps, equipment, effort, provenance, and save permission. Missing required information is identified beside the field. User-authored recipes carry a clear provenance label; they do not inherit a professional-review badge. `POST` and `STORY` present “View recipe” only when the attachment is ready. `RECIPE` shows the exact attached version.

## Production sequence
The picker reads `GET /v1/recipes` and `GET /v1/saved-recipes`, then shows only server-eligible publication sources. `PATCH /v1/post-drafts/{draftId}` submits `attachment` with exactly one recipe-version reference, owned plan reference, or structured `personalRecipe`, plus `allowRecipeSaves` and `If-Match`. The server checks source rights, derives actual review provenance, validates fields, then creates an immutable version/reference and emits `post.attachment_changed`. Client rights/review labels are claims to verify, not authority. Publication rechecks eligibility and recall status. Later attachment replacement uses `PATCH /v1/posts/{postId}` with `If-Match`, creates a new attachment identity, and affects future views/saves only.

## Data and API contracts
`RecipeAttachment(id,postId,recipeVersionId,sourceKind,savePolicy,grantVersion,createdAt,replacedAt)` references an immutable `RecipeVersion`. Source kinds distinguish reviewed catalog, author recipe, and authorized derivative. The unique active attachment per post invariant is enforced by a database constraint. An attachment does not contain source media URLs, comments, private preferences, or cooking-session notes. `ATTACHMENT_NOT_PUBLISHABLE`, `RECIPE_RECALLED`, and `RECIPE_INCOMPLETE` are typed validation errors. A privately saved third-party copy is not attachable merely because it exists in the author’s cookbook.

## Permissions and lifecycle
Reading an attachment through a post requires current post access. Saving additionally requires the active grant policy in F30. Third-party saved recipe versions grant private use only; V1 allows an own photo/note and scoped source link, but no republication of the source recipe text. A catalog source may be attachable only where its explicit distribution rights permit. Replacing or withdrawing an attachment stops new access through it; earlier authorized private copies persist unless recalled for safety or legal deletion.

## Failure and offline behavior
Recipe drafts can be edited locally, but attachment eligibility and publishing require the server. Retry attachment creation with the same idempotency key. If the source changes or becomes unavailable, keep the author’s unpublished draft and offer removal or a different eligible recipe. Do not silently substitute a newer recipe version. Unknown photo contents never populate food facts automatically.

## Integration and dependencies
Depends on catalog validation, source-rights metadata, private cookbook provenance, F30 grants, and F31 drafts. F01 adapts only an accessible version; it must preserve hard exclusions and original-version attribution.

## Execution plan
Implement immutable references and attachability filtering, then structured entry and preview, then grant-aware replacement. Instrument validation reasons and attachment completion without logging full recipes or preferences.

## Acceptance and release gates
Test replacement racing a save, withdrawn source rights before publishing, incomplete recipes, recalled versions, and attempts to attach private third-party copies. `social.attachments.enabled` disables new attachments while existing authorized copies remain available. Release requires correct provenance labels and proof that no original media enters recipe snapshots.
